# Section 7 Solutions

These are the answers to the [Section 7 Review](/learning-paths/career-leadership/section-7-review)'s Knowledge Check. If you haven't attempted the three scenarios yet, do that first.

## Scenario 1: The Excluded QA Team

**Correct answer**: Reframe testing findings in terms of what product actually cares about (user experience, business impact) and seek earlier involvement at the requirements stage, per [Working with Product and Developers](/learning-paths/career-leadership/working-with-product-and-developers).

**Explanation**: This is the module's own opening scenario restated — an adversarial framing and late-only involvement erodes trust and leads to exactly this kind of exclusion.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Escalating the exclusion as unfair, without addressing the underlying relationship pattern, doesn't fix the actual cause — the fix is changing how and when QA engages, not asserting a right to be included.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "interest-based framing and early involvement rebuild trust" was understood as the fix, not just "insist on being included."

## Scenario 2: The Unowned Pipeline

**Correct answer**: Proactive, joint diagnosis treating the pipeline as genuinely shared territory, per [Working with DevOps and Stakeholder Management](/learning-paths/career-leadership/working-with-devops-and-stakeholder-management) — not waiting for either side to claim sole ownership.

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — shared infrastructure needs explicit cross-functional ownership, since assuming it's someone else's job leaves it unresolved indefinitely.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Escalating to management to assign clear single ownership might resolve ambiguity but misses the actual insight — the problem often needs both groups' complementary expertise working together, not exclusive ownership by one side.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "genuinely shared ownership, not exclusive assignment" was understood as the model, not just "someone needs to own this."

## Scenario 3: The Over-Explained Answer

**Correct answer**: Lead with the direct answer first, then supporting reasoning, per [Executive Communication](/learning-paths/career-leadership/executive-communication) — "Yes, with one caveat..." rather than a detailed explanation building toward a conclusion.

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — under executive time pressure, reasoning that arrives before the answer reads as evasive or unprepared.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply being more concise while still explaining first, then concluding, doesn't fully fix the issue — the order needs to invert, not just compress.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "answer first, reasoning second" was understood as a structural inversion, not just "be brief."
