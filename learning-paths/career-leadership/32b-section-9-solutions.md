# Section 9 Solutions

These are the answers to the [Section 9 Review](/learning-paths/career-leadership/section-9-review)'s Knowledge Check. If you haven't attempted the two scenarios yet, do that first.

## Scenario 1: The Overstated Announcement

**Correct answer**: Pilot the tool on a bounded, well-understood task first, measure actual impact, and communicate verified findings rather than projected capability, per [AI-Assisted QA Leadership and Automation Transformation](/learning-paths/career-leadership/ai-assisted-qa-leadership-and-automation-transformation).

**Explanation**: This is the module's own opening scenario restated — an overstated promise that later has to be walked back costs more trust than a modest, evidence-based claim would have.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply announcing a smaller, still-unverified claim doesn't fully fix the issue — the actual fix is piloting and measuring before making any adoption claim, not just moderating the claim's scope.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "pilot and measure before claiming capability" was understood as the fix, not just "don't overpromise."

## Scenario 2: The Reluctant Team

**Correct answer**: Deliberately, visibly model blameless language in defect discussions going forward, explicitly separating accountability from blame, per [Engineering Culture and Innovation in Testing](/learning-paths/career-leadership/engineering-culture-and-innovation-in-testing).

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — even one unintentional blame incident teaches a team to conceal rather than disclose, and rebuilding trust requires consistent, visible counter-modeling over time.

**Alternative approaches considered**: A single apology or one-time statement about valuing honesty doesn't rebuild trust — the fix requires sustained, visible behavior change over an extended period, not a one-time gesture.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "culture is rebuilt through sustained, visible leadership behavior, not a single statement" was understood as the fix.
