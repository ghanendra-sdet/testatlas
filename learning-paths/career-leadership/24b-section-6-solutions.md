# Section 6 Solutions

These are the answers to the [Section 6 Review](/learning-paths/career-leadership/section-6-review)'s Knowledge Check. If you haven't attempted the four scenarios yet, do that first.

## Scenario 1: The Checklist Rejection

**Correct answer**: The hiring process should assess judgment and reasoning through scenario-based exercises, not a checklist of specific tools, per [Hiring and Interviewing Test Engineers](/learning-paths/career-leadership/hiring-and-interviewing-test-engineers).

**Explanation**: This is the module's own opening scenario restated — specific tool knowledge can be taught quickly, but sound underlying reasoning transfers across tools and is what actually predicts on-the-job success.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply expanding the checklist to cover more tools doesn't fix the underlying issue — the format itself, checklist versus scenario-based reasoning, is the actual problem.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "assess transferable judgment, not memorized or tool-specific knowledge" was understood as the fix, not just "the checklist needs more items."

## Scenario 2: The Reconstructed Review

**Correct answer**: A running, specific record kept throughout the year is missing, per [Career Development and Performance Reviews](/learning-paths/career-leadership/career-development-and-performance-reviews) — reviews should synthesize ongoing attention, not be reconstructed from memory shortly before they're due.

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — a review based on vague, recent impressions misses most of a year's actual work.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply spending more time writing the review right before it's due doesn't fix the underlying gap — the missing piece is ongoing tracking throughout the year, not more effort at review time.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "ongoing record-keeping, not better annual writing" was understood as the fix.

## Scenario 3: The Six-Month Delay

**Correct answer**: The cost is a specific, avoidable pattern repeating unaddressed for months, and feedback losing its specificity and impact by the time it's finally delivered, per [Giving Feedback](/learning-paths/career-leadership/giving-feedback).

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — feedback given close to the relevant moment is far more actionable than feedback delayed until a formal review.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Assuming the eventual September feedback is just as effective as timely feedback, only later, misses the actual cost — both the delay itself and the loss of specificity matter.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "proximity to the instance is what makes feedback actionable" was understood as the core lesson, not just "feedback should eventually be given."

## Scenario 4: The Forgotten Goal

**Correct answer**: Structured, regular coaching check-ins explicitly revisiting the stated goal, per [Coaching](/learning-paths/career-leadership/coaching) — a goal mentioned once and never structurally revisited effectively goes nowhere.

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — coaching requires ongoing, deliberate attention to a stated goal, not a single conversation.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Assuming the team member should simply raise it again themselves if it matters to them shifts responsibility away from the leader's own coaching practice — the fix is a structural check-in habit, not waiting for the report to re-raise it.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "coaching requires ongoing, structured revisiting of a stated goal" was understood as the fix, not just "have more conversations eventually."
