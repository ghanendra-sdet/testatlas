# Section 8 Solutions

These are the answers to the [Section 8 Review](/learning-paths/career-leadership/section-8-review)'s Knowledge Check. If you haven't attempted the three scenarios yet, do that first.

## Scenario 1: The Imported Enterprise Playbook

**Correct answer**: Assess the startup's actual stage and apply a much lighter, risk-focused approach matched to it, per [QA in Startups vs. Enterprises](/learning-paths/career-leadership/qa-in-startups-vs-enterprises) — not the enterprise playbook that made sense in a different context.

**Explanation**: This is the module's own opening scenario restated — heavyweight process at a stage where speed and learning matter most stifles the startup's actual advantage without a corresponding quality benefit.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Gradually easing the enterprise process rather than fundamentally rethinking it for the new context still risks retaining unnecessary overhead — the fix is genuine reassessment of what this specific stage needs, not a lighter version of the same playbook.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "match rigor to actual stage, not personal prior experience" was understood as the fix, not just "startups need less process."

## Scenario 2: The Inconvenient Timezone

**Correct answer**: Shift to asynchronous-first coordination and rotate any necessary synchronous meeting times fairly, per [Distributed Teams and Scaling Automation](/learning-paths/career-leadership/distributed-teams-and-scaling-automation).

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — co-located coordination practices don't scale fairly across distribution without genuine redesign.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply asking the newest timezone to adjust their schedule to accommodate the existing meeting time addresses the symptom but not the underlying fairness and coordination-design problem.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "redesign coordination for distribution, don't just stretch co-located practice" was understood as the fix.

## Scenario 3: The Disconnected Center of Excellence

**Correct answer**: A rotating staffing model keeping CoE members connected to real product-team work, and an evidence requirement that standards be piloted before broad adoption, per [Building Centers of Excellence](/learning-paths/career-leadership/building-centers-of-excellence).

**Explanation**: This is that module's own opening scenario restated — standards designed in isolation from real practice tend to be technically sound but practically disconnected, producing low genuine adoption.

**Alternative approaches considered**: Simply mandating stronger compliance with the existing standards doesn't fix the underlying issue — the standards themselves need to be grounded in demonstrated, piloted practice, not enforced more strictly as originally designed.

**Real-world reasoning**: Tests whether "connect the CoE to real, piloted practice" was understood as the fix, not just "improve standard enforcement."
