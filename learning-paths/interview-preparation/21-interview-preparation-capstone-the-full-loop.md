# Interview Preparation Capstone: The Full Loop

**Prerequisites**: The entire Interview Preparation path — every module in Sections 1 through 5, and both mock interview tracks in Section 6.
**Leads to**: This completes Interview Preparation v1.0.

Every module in this path taught one technique or one round type. This capstone doesn't teach anything new — it's the complete, realistic loop, run once, continuously, the way a real interview day actually happens: no pausing between rounds to review a module, no picking the round you feel strongest in first. Set aside 45 uninterrupted minutes, and treat what follows as the real thing.

## Before You Start

Choose your primary technical track now — Manual/Test-Design or SDET/Automation — the way a real interview loop is usually shaped around the specific role you're interviewing for. You'll still get one cross-domain moment regardless of which track you choose, since almost every real QA interview touches at least one domain outside a candidate's primary focus.

---

## Round 1 — Behavioral Warm-Up (5 minutes)

**Interviewer**: "Walk me through a project you're proud of, and your specific role in it."

*Answer fully, out loud or in writing, before continuing. No model answer this time — evaluate your own answer against the checklist at the end.*

---

## Round 2 — Technical Deep-Dive (12 minutes)

**If Manual/Test-Design track — Interviewer**: "Design test cases for a hotel-booking date-range picker that must not allow a check-out date before the check-in date, with a maximum stay of 14 nights."

**If SDET/Automation track — Interviewer**: "Your team's regression suite takes 45 minutes to run and has started producing occasional, unexplained failures. Walk me through how you'd approach this."

*Answer fully before continuing, whichever prompt matches your chosen track.*

---

## Round 3 — Open-Ended Scenario (10 minutes)

**Interviewer**: "How would you test a self-checkout kiosk at a grocery store?"

*Remember: clarify scope first, identify the worst realistic outcome, then prioritize. Answer fully before continuing.*

---

## Round 4 — Cross-Domain Moment (5 minutes)

**Interviewer**: "This kiosk you just described syncs inventory data to a central system in real time. What would you want to know about how that sync is tested?"

*This connects your Round 3 answer to a database/integration concern. Answer briefly and at a correctly-scoped depth before continuing.*

---

## Round 5 — Closing (5 minutes)

**Interviewer**: "Do you have any questions for me, and is there anything about your background you'd like to add that we haven't covered?"

*A real interview's closing round evaluates genuine engagement, not just information — answer as you actually would.*

## Self-Assessment: Score Your Own Full Loop

Now, and only now, work through this checklist against everything you answered above, using [Interview Feedback and Improvement Checklist](/learning-paths/interview-preparation/interview-feedback-and-improvement-checklist)'s own format — name specific rounds and specific gaps, not general impressions:

- **Round recognition**: did you answer each round in the shape that round actually called for — structured STAR for behavioral, method-first for technical, scope-then-priority for the open-ended scenario?
- **Visible reasoning**: across every round, was your reasoning stated, or did you jump straight to conclusions?
- **Specificity**: were your examples and answers concrete, or did any drift into generalities?
- **Depth calibration**: in Round 4, did you answer at an appropriately brief, correctly-scoped depth, or did you either overreach or disengage?
- **Composure**: did you pause deliberately before harder questions, or fill silence with filler?

For any round that felt weaker than the others, name the specific gap and the specific module from this path that addresses it — exactly the discipline [Interview Feedback and Improvement Checklist](/learning-paths/interview-preparation/interview-feedback-and-improvement-checklist) taught, now applied to your own full, timed performance rather than an isolated round.
